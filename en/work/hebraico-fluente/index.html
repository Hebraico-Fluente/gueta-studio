Hebraico Fluente

My own Hebrew school for Brazilians: a complete platform, built from scratch.

See the platform ↗hebraicofluente.com.br

01The challenge

Learning Hebrew starts with a new alphabet, read from right to left, with vowel marks.

I was already teaching Hebrew in the classroom. The challenge was to bring the method to a platform where students study on their own, with no teacher at the board, and don't get stuck at the very first letter.

Because it's my own project, it's also where I test, with real students, everything I later bring to clients' sites: accounts, payments and screens that explain themselves.

02Design decisions, and the reason for each

  1. The order of the letters follows the method.

    The 22 letters don't come in dictionary order. The course follows the lesson plan in the teacher's guide בא לי עברית!: 7 modules and 22 lessons, in which each new letter unlocks real words early on.

  2. No word with a letter the student hasn't seen yet.

    It's not a guideline, it's a system rule: if an exercise uses a letter that comes later, the platform doesn't even get published. Students never run into an impossible example.

  3. Bridge words instead of a teacher.

    In the classroom, each letter is born inside a word the student already knows. On the platform, every module opens the same way: the borrowed word, what it means and the space for the new letter.

  4. A map, not a list of lessons.

    Progress appears as a path, letter by letter, up to the first reading. It makes you want to take the next step, and students always know where they left off.

03What was built

For students

  • Personal account and journey map
  • Interactive reading exercises
  • Review checkpoints at the end of modules
  • Access unlocked automatically after payment

For the school

  • Pix and card payments
  • A courses page and a page for each course
  • Admin area

Behind the scenes

  • Course content as validated data, with automatic rules
  • Automated tests before every release
  • The same code ready for English and Spanish versions
